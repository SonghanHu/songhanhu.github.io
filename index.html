I'm finishing my M.S. in Data Science at Harvard (May '26), cross-registered at MIT, after graduating first in my class at UCI in Mathematics and Quantitative Economics. I build ML systems that have to work in the real world — where the data is messy, the deadlines are real, and nobody cares how elegant your loss function is if the thing doesn't ship.

What I Think About

My research sits at the intersection of quantitative finance and applied machine learning. I'm drawn to problems where you need statistical rigor to survive contact with chaotic, high-dimensional reality — rates curve construction, time-series forecasting, multimodal pricing for things that don't have a ticker symbol.

Lately I've been deep into agentic AI systems — building autonomous agents that can reason, plan, and actually do things. I spend a probably-unhealthy amount of time wiring up claw agents that automate tasks I'm too lazy to do myself. The dream is systems that think for you while you sleep. We're not there yet, but we're close enough that it's fun to try.

I care about the full stack of a model's life: the idea, the math, the engineering, the deployment, and whether it still works on a Tuesday three months from now. If it only lives in a notebook, it doesn't count.

When I'm Not Working

I take my hobbies the way I take my models — obsess over the process, track the metrics, blame variance when things go wrong.

Golf is the current grind. Shooting in the low 90s, which means I'm exactly good enough to know how bad I am. The feedback loop is addicting though — it's basically gradient descent but outside.

Badminton is where the competitive energy goes. Skiing is where the send-it energy goes. Very different vibes, both essential.

Poker is the quiet obsession. I'm a solid, disciplined, +EV player — which is a fancy way of saying I fold a lot and occasionally feel very smart about it. The game is honestly the best training ground for thinking under uncertainty, which is also what I do for a living.
