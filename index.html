I'm finishing my M.S. in Data Science at Harvard (May '26), cross-registered at MIT, after graduating first in my class at UCI in Mathematics and Quantitative Economics. I build ML systems that have to work in the real world — where the data is messy, the deadlines are real, and nobody cares how elegant your loss function is if the thing doesn't ship.

Research & work

My work sits at the intersection of quantitative finance and applied machine learning. I'm drawn to problems where you need statistical rigor to survive contact with chaotic, high-dimensional reality — rates curve construction, time-series forecasting, multimodal pricing for things that don't have a ticker symbol.

Lately I've been deep into agentic AI systems: building autonomous agents that can reason, plan, and actually do things. I spend a lot of time wiring up agents that automate tasks I'm too lazy to do myself. I care about the full stack of a model's life — the idea, the math, the engineering, the deployment, and whether it still works on a Tuesday three months from now. If it only lives in a notebook, it doesn't count.
